# Inflation and Unemployment in Italy: A Data Analysis (2015–2026)

## Overview

This project analyzes monthly inflation and unemployment trends in Italy from 2015 to 2026.

The main objective is to understand how these two economic indicators changed over time and whether there was a meaningful relationship between them.

## Research Question

Can inflation and unemployment help tell the story of Italy's economy?

## Data

The project uses monthly data from Eurostat:

- Harmonised Index of Consumer Prices (HICP) — annual inflation rate
- Unemployment rate — seasonally adjusted

Period: August 2015 – July 2026

Country: Italy

## Key Findings

- Average inflation over the period was approximately 2.16%.
- Average unemployment was approximately 8.97%.
- Inflation reached a high of 12.6% in October 2022.
- Inflation reached a low of approximately -1.1% in September 2020.
- The Pearson correlation between inflation and unemployment was approximately -0.33.

The correlation indicates a weak negative linear relationship. It should not be interpreted as evidence of causation.

## Economic Interpretation

The data shows several important periods:

- The COVID-19 period was associated with very low inflation and elevated unemployment.
- Inflation increased sharply during 2021–2022, reaching its highest level in October 2022.
- The post-2022 period shows a gradual decline in inflation from its peak.

These movements reflect major economic shocks and changing economic conditions rather than a simple one-to-one relationship between inflation and unemployment.
